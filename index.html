Community Cards: UW

A conversation game that helps first-year and transfer students move past small talk at orientation events.

Tier 1What surprised you most?
Tier 2A moment you didn't belong.
Tier 3 · ReflectWhat's a fear you brought with you to UW?

The problem

New students are told to get involved, but most icebreakers stop at names and majors. Groups rarely get permission to go deeper, so people leave events without real connections.

Design decisions

  • Three tiers of depth. Cards move from Perceive to Connect to Reflect, so a group can raise the stakes at its own pace instead of jumping straight to vulnerable questions.
  • A short personality test first. Players choose an MBTI-inspired, interest-based, or values-based test, and the result shapes which cards they draw.
  • Built into events students already attend. The game is designed for Dawg Days, FIGS, RSO meetings, and dorm events, not as one more app to download.

Social Capital Map

A campus map that shows students, first-generation students especially, which resource to try first based on what they want to build.

The problem

Campus offices, mentor programs, and career events already exist. The hard part is knowing which one fits right now, and which one won't feel intimidating to walk into first. A directory of every office doesn't answer that.

Design decisions

  • Start from the student's goal. Students pick Explore, Interview, Confidence, or Internship, and the interface brings forward what fits that goal.
  • Order by stakes, not by department. The route starts with low-pressure places, then peer and alumni stories, then practice in public.
  • Turn each opportunity into a place. Every resource has a physical spot, like the welcome desk or the peer table, so students know where to go.

Drama Studio

An AI short-drama studio. Paste a script, and it plans the shots, casts the characters, generates the clips, and cuts a finished vertical episode.

Script → episode

The problem

AI video models only make clips a few seconds long, and each clip can change a character's face or outfit. A short drama needs dozens of clips that look like the same people, and creators need to know what a run will cost before they start it.

Design decisions

  • One canvas for the whole pipeline. Script, characters, shots, and the finished episode sit on a node canvas, next to a storyboard list and a preview player.
  • Consistency from reference images. Each character gets one reference image from a fixed description that is reused word for word in every prompt. Keyframes are made from those references and then animated, which keeps faces stable in motion.
  • Free to try. With no API keys, the whole flow runs offline with placeholder frames, ffmpeg animatics, and system voices. Each step can then be switched to a real model.
  • Cost before you click. The studio estimates every batch before it runs and tracks spend per project.
  • Works in and outside China. Models run through fal.ai or Volcengine Ark, and scripts are split into shots by Claude, Doubao, or an offline rule-based splitter.

Go-Global Studio 出海方案工作室

A consulting service that takes Chinese factories from "we want to sell overseas" to a working online store, with the first stage automated.

4 stages × 3 packages

The problem

Many factories sell only through distributors. Selling direct overseas means picking a market, setting up a US company, building a store, and running it, and most have done none of these. A first market assessment takes hours of research for every prospect.

Design decisions

  • Four stages, three packages. Assessment, company setup, store build, and operations are sold as Validate, Standard, or Full-service, so a client can start small and upgrade after real sales.
  • Automate only the first stage. A Claude Code skill turns a client's intake form into a draft assessment report. The other stages stay manual until the service proves itself.
  • Research that can't be faked. The skill looks up 3 to 5 real comparable brands and must not invent a brand, URL, or price. Anything the client didn't answer stays marked "to confirm" (待确认).
  • A person signs off. The report is a draft the consultant reads before anything reaches a client, and the legal and tax disclaimer has to survive every edit.
  • A client board, not a portal. A Notion or Airtable board tracks each client's stage and next step. Clients only receive reports, store links, and email.

Tested end to end on Case 0, the MonsterDangDang store below, as an internal case study.

MonsterDangDang Store 怪兽当当独立站

The first version of a bilingual online store for MonsterDangDang, a Shenzhen maker of tablet keyboards that had only sold through distributors.

The problem

The company sold through distributors and gift buyers and had never run its own store. It wanted a site that sells for the company directly, in English for the US and in Chinese at home, without listing all 42 models.

Design decisions

  • 8 flagship models, not 42. The store features 8 models the client picked, so each gets a full page with colors, specs, and compatible tablets.
  • Lead with the standouts. The homepage puts the AI keyboard and the solar-charging model up front. The later market assessment found competitors weren't stressing either feature.
  • One switch for two languages. Every product is written in English and Chinese, and the 中文 / EN choice is remembered from page to page.
  • A full cart without taking money yet. Shoppers can add items and check out, which sends an order request for manual payment follow-up until a payment provider is connected.
  • No framework. Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with the catalog in one file, so the store can become a template for the studio's store-build stage.

Prices on the site are placeholder estimates until the factory confirms real pricing.

Token Gateway

One self-hosted endpoint in front of OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek, Gemini, and other model providers, with per-key budgets and every request metered in tokens and dollars.

The problem

Teams that use several AI model providers juggle separate keys, two incompatible API formats, and bills that arrive after the money is spent. There's no simple way to give each person a budget, or to keep working when one provider goes down.

Design decisions

  • Speak both formats. Apps can call in OpenAI or Anthropic format, and the gateway translates requests, streams, and tool calls when a provider speaks the other one. OpenAI SDK apps can use Claude, and Claude Code can use DeepSeek.
  • Keys with budgets. Each key can have a dollar budget, per-minute request and token limits, a list of allowed models, and an expiry date. Keys are stored as hashes and shown only once.
  • Route around failures. Channels are tried by priority and share traffic by weight. A channel that keeps failing cools down for 30 seconds, doubling up to 32 times.
  • Meter everything, store no prompts. Every request logs input, output, and cache tokens and its cost from an editable price table. Prompt and reply text are never stored.
  • Easy to try. A demo mode runs the gateway against a built-in mock provider with sample keys and two weeks of generated history.

BTC Trading Assistant

A dashboard that lets someone test a Bitcoin trading signal against past data before risking money on it.

12345
Data → regime → signal → backtest

The problem

Retail traders often act on a price move without checking whether the same signal held up before, or what kind of market they are trading in.

Design decisions

  • Backtest before you trade. A backtesting engine scores simple signals over a time range the user picks.
  • Show the market's mood. Regime detection tells the user what kind of market a signal is being tested in.
  • Safe by default. Paper trading and the Robinhood connection are off unless the user turns them on, and credentials stay in environment variables.
  • Keep working when data fails. If the live market-data API is down, the dashboard falls back to stored BTC and ETH history.
